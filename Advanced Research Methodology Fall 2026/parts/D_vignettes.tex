\chapter{Five Worked IT Studies}\label{app:vignettes}
%=====================================================================

Five studies carried from question to write-up, one per major design. They show
the whole chain in one place, which no individual chapter can.

\begin{alertbox}[Everything here is constructed]
These studies were written for this book. The settings are realistic and the
methods are correct, but \textbf{every number is invented}. Nothing in this
appendix may be cited as a finding, and no result here is attributed to any
real study. They are shapes to imitate, not evidence.
\end{alertbox}

\section{Vignette 1 --- Systematic Review}

\begin{vignette}[Testing techniques for microservice architectures]
\textbf{Question.} What techniques are used to test microservice-based systems,
and which have been evaluated in industrial settings?

\medskip
\textbf{Type.} Systematic review, descriptive and evaluative
(\chref{questions}). Chosen over a mapping study because enough primary studies
exist to answer a specific question about industrial evaluation.

\medskip
\textbf{PICOC.} P: microservice-based systems. I: testing techniques.
C: each other, and testing of monolithic systems where reported. O: defects
found, coverage achieved, effort. C: industrial versus academic settings.

\medskip
\textbf{Protocol} (C3), fixed before searching. Databases: IEEE Xplore, ACM DL,
Scopus, SpringerLink. Range 2014--2025. Quasi-gold standard of 7 papers;
the string reached full recall on the fourth iteration.

\medskip
\textbf{PRISMA counts.} 3,180 identified; 1,042 duplicates removed; 2,138
title-and-abstract screened, 1,905 excluded; 233 full texts assessed, 178
excluded --- 61 not microservice-specific, 54 no testing technique, 39 not a
study, 24 unobtainable; 26 added by snowballing; \textbf{81 included}.
$\kappa = 0.71$ on a 15\% double-screened sample.

\medskip
\textbf{Appraisal.} C6 applied; quality used as a \emph{moderator}, not an
exclusion threshold, decided in the protocol.

\medskip
\textbf{Synthesis.} Narrative and thematic --- effect sizes were reported in
only 9 of 81 studies, so meta-analysis was impossible. That impossibility is
reported as a finding about the field's measurement practices, not hidden.

\medskip
\textbf{Result.} Six technique families. 68 of 81 studies evaluated only in
laboratory or simulated settings; 13 in industry, of which 9 were single-company
experience reports. The gap: contract testing is the most-proposed technique
and among the least industrially evaluated.

\medskip
\textbf{Threats} (\chref{validity}). \emph{Construct}: ``microservice'' is
inconsistently defined across studies, so inclusion required a judgement that
two screeners applied with $\kappa = 0.71$ --- adequate, not excellent.
\emph{Conclusion}: no pooling was possible, so all synthesis is narrative.
\emph{External}: English-language venues only.

\medskip
\textbf{What it becomes.} A journal paper in its own right, \emph{and} the
literature review chapter, \emph{and} the evidenced gap in the synopsis
(\chref{synopsis}). One piece of work, three uses --- which is how a PhD
publication plan should be built (\chref{publishing}).
\end{vignette}

\section{Vignette 2 --- Controlled Experiment}

\begin{vignette}[Does an LLM review assistant change defect detection?]
\textbf{Question.} Causal: does an LLM-based review assistant change the number
of seeded defects a reviewer identifies, and does the effect depend on the time
budget?

\medskip
\textbf{Design} (C7). $2\times2$ within-subjects: assistant present or absent,
time box 10 or 25 minutes. 34 professional developers, each completing four
reviews. Latin square over four equivalent code artefacts, so technique never
meets the same artefact twice in the same position (\chref{experiments}).

\medskip
\textbf{Unit of analysis.} The \emph{review}, 136 in total, nested within 34
developers and 4 artefacts. Analysed with crossed random effects --- not as
$N=136$ independent observations.

\medskip
\textbf{Power} (\chref{sampling}). SESOI set at 1 additional defect; baseline
$s = 2.5$, so $d = 0.40$. Within-subjects with an assumed correlation of 0.6
gives 0.83 power at 34 participants. Pre-registered (C12) before recruitment.

\medskip
\textbf{Measures.} Primary: seeded defects correctly identified, out of 12.
Secondary: false positives raised --- included precisely because the review
review in \chref{appraisal} showed omitting it inflates apparent benefit.

\medskip
\textbf{Analysis, pre-specified.}
\begin{lstlisting}[language=Rlang]
m <- lmer(found ~ assistant * timebox + (1 + assistant | dev) + (1 | artefact),
          data = reviews)
\end{lstlisting}

\medskip
\textbf{Result.} Main effect of assistant $+0.9$ defects (95\% CI $[0.2,
1.6]$). Interaction with time box: at 25 minutes $+1.6$ $[0.7, 2.5]$; at 10
minutes $+0.1$ $[-0.8, 1.0]$. False positives rose by 2.3 $[1.4, 3.2]$ with the
assistant, independent of time box.

\medskip
\textbf{Interpretation.} The assistant helps when there is time to evaluate its
suggestions and not otherwise, while adding false positives throughout. Under
tight time budgets it is plausibly net harmful --- a claim the secondary
measure makes possible and that the primary measure alone would have missed.

\medskip
\textbf{Threats.} \emph{Construct}: seeded defects are not naturally occurring
defects. \emph{External}: 200--400 line artefacts, not legacy systems.
\emph{Internal}: mitigated by randomisation and counterbalancing. \emph{Conclusion}:
powered for $d = 0.40$; smaller effects would be missed.
\end{vignette}

\section{Vignette 3 --- Design Science}

\begin{vignette}[Federated learning across hospitals with correlated missingness]
\textbf{Problem class.} Clinical prediction across institutions that cannot
share records, where missingness patterns differ by institution because
recording practices differ.

\medskip
\textbf{Positioning} (\chref{designscience}). Gregor--Hevner:
\emph{improvement} --- a known problem, a new solution. Federated learning
exists; handling hospital-correlated missingness within it does not.

\medskip
\textbf{Artefact type.} A \emph{method} --- a missingness-aware aggregation
rule --- demonstrated by an \emph{instantiation}. The method is the
contribution; the system is the evidence it is realisable.

\medskip
\textbf{Design knowledge sentence.} ``If the artefact were deleted, what
remains is: when missingness is correlated with the partition, proximal
regularisation penalises the local adaptation that recovers it, and an
aggregation rule weighting by per-feature completeness restores most of the
lost accuracy.''

\medskip
\textbf{Objectives} (DSRM step 2, fixed before building). O1: accuracy within
2 points of centralised training at realistic skew. O2: no raw record leaves
its institution. O3: deployable on hardware the partner sites have.

\medskip
\textbf{Evaluation} (FEDS). Dominant risk is \emph{technical efficacy}, so:
three rounds of artificial formative evaluation on public datasets with
synthetic missingness; then summative artificial comparison against FedAvg and
FedProx on two clinical datasets, ten seeds each with intervals; then one
naturalistic summative case study at a single partner hospital
(\chref{casestudy}) to check O3.

\medskip
\textbf{Result.} 1.4 points below centralised at high skew, against 4.8 for
FedAvg and 5.6 for FedProx. The naturalistic study found O3 satisfied but
revealed an unanticipated requirement: site staff needed a way to audit what
left the building, which no baseline provides. That requirement is reported as
design knowledge in its own right.

\medskip
\textbf{Threats.} \emph{Conclusion}: two datasets, so the result is not shown to
generalise across clinical domains. \emph{External}: one partner site.
\emph{Construct}: synthetic missingness in formative evaluation may not
resemble real recording practice --- which is exactly why the naturalistic
stage was included.
\end{vignette}

\section{Vignette 4 --- Survey and PLS-SEM}

\begin{vignette}[Determinants of e-governance system adoption]
\textbf{Question.} Relational: which UTAUT2 constructs predict intention to use
a provincial e-governance portal among municipal officers?

\medskip
\textbf{Instrument} (\chref{survey}). UTAUT2 adopted \emph{unmodified}, four
items per construct, seven-point scale. Adopting rather than adapting keeps the
validation evidence, and is stated as the reason.

\medskip
\textbf{Sampling.} Population: municipal officers in the province. No sampling
frame exists, so: stratified purposive across three district types, recruited
through departmental contacts. \textbf{Reported as a non-probability sample};
no margin of error claimed (\chref{survey}).

\medskip
\textbf{Common method bias, designed out.} Predictors measured at time 1,
intention at time 2 four weeks later. Temporal separation is procedural, and
far stronger than Harman's test after the fact (\chref{sem}).

\medskip
\textbf{Funnel.} 640 invited, 411 started, 352 completed, 338 usable. Wave
analysis found no difference between early and late responders on the primary
constructs.

\medskip
\textbf{Measurement model, assessed first.} Loadings all above 0.71. Composite
reliability 0.83--0.91 --- none above 0.95. AVE 0.58--0.74. HTMT: all below
0.85 \emph{except} effort expectancy against facilitating conditions at 0.89.

\medskip
\textbf{The decision that matters.} That HTMT failure was not reported and
ignored. The two constructs were merged into a single ``perceived ease of
access'' construct, the model was respecified, and \textbf{the change was
disclosed as data-driven}, with the results reported as exploratory for that
path (\chref{prereg}).

\medskip
\textbf{Structural model.} 5,000 bootstrap resamples. Performance expectancy
$\rightarrow$ intention $\beta = 0.41$ $[0.29, 0.52]$; perceived ease of access
$\beta = 0.22$ $[0.09, 0.34]$; social influence $\beta = 0.07$ $[-0.04, 0.19]$
--- interval includes zero, reported as no detectable effect rather than as
non-significant. $R^2 = 0.47$.

\medskip
\textbf{Threats.} \emph{Internal}: cross-sectional despite temporal separation;
arrows are theory, not design warrant. \emph{External}: one province.
\emph{Construct}: a construct merge, disclosed. \emph{Conclusion}: bootstrap
intervals reported throughout.
\end{vignette}

\section{Vignette 5 --- Repository Mining and Machine Learning}

\begin{vignette}[Does defect prediction survive an honest validation scheme?]
\textbf{Question.} Evaluative and replicative: do published defect prediction
results hold under grouped and temporal validation?

\medskip
\textbf{Why it is a contribution.} \chref{contribution} would classify this as
a \emph{replication}. \chref{mleval} supplies the reason it is worth doing: the
leakage literature suggests reported advantages may not survive correction.

\medskip
\textbf{Data.} 30 open-source Java projects. Filters applied and reported:
minimum 3 contributors, minimum 200 commits, issue tracker in use, bot accounts
removed (\chref{quasiexp}). 1,247 bot-authored commits excluded.

\medskip
\textbf{The four corrections.}
\begin{tight}
  \item \emph{Group split.} Train on 24 projects, test on 6, rotated --- rather
    than randomly splitting files, which lets the model memorise per-project
    base rates.
  \item \emph{Temporal labelling.} Metrics at a fixed snapshot; labels from
    defects found in the following 12 months only.
  \item \emph{Pipeline-encapsulated preprocessing.} Scaling and imputation fit
    inside each training fold.
  \item \emph{Baselines added.} Predict-majority, and logistic regression on
    file size alone.
\end{tight}

\medskip
\textbf{Result.} Under random file-level splitting, gradient boosting reaches
0.89 AUC. Under the corrected scheme: gradient boosting 0.67, logistic
regression 0.66, size-only baseline 0.64. Friedman across the 30 projects finds
no significant difference between gradient boosting and logistic regression;
both exceed the size-only baseline (\chref{mleval}).

\medskip
\textbf{Interpretation.} Most of the reported advantage of complex models in
this task is an artefact of the validation scheme, and most of the remaining
signal is size. This is a negative result, and it is the contribution.

\medskip
\textbf{Artefacts.} Container with pinned digest, all seeds, both validation
schemes runnable from one command so a reader can reproduce the drop
themselves. Deposited with a DOI (C13).

\medskip
\textbf{Threats.} \emph{Construct}: \texttt{bug}-labelled issues measure
labelling practice as well as defects; a 200-issue manual validation found 82\%
agreement, reported. \emph{External}: open-source Java only. \emph{Internal}:
observational, so no causal claim is made about metrics and defects.
\end{vignette}

\begin{conceptbox}[What the five have in common]
Read across them and the same six moves appear every time, whatever the design:

\begin{tightnum}
  \item The question is stated in a form that could come out either way.
  \item The design is chosen against a named alternative, for a stated reason.
  \item The sample size is justified before collection.
  \item The analysis is specified before the data exists.
  \item A measure or comparison is included specifically because omitting it
    would flatter the result.
  \item The threats section names the study's own worst problem first.
\end{tightnum}

\medskip
Those six are the course. Everything else is the detail of a particular method.
\end{conceptbox}
