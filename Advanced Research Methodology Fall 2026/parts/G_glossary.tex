\chapter{Glossary}\label{app:glossary}
%=====================================================================

Terms as this book uses them. Where a term is contested or used differently
across traditions, that is noted rather than smoothed over.

\textbf{Term} & \textbf{Meaning} \\

Abduction & Reasoning from a surprising observation to the best explanation of
  it. Generates hypotheses; does not test them (\chref{philosophy}) \\
Ablation & Removing one component of a method at a time to identify which earns
  the reported gain (\chref{mleval}) \\
Analytic generalisation & Generalising from findings to a theory applicable to
  other cases sharing the relevant conditions. What a case study offers; not
  statistical generalisation (\chref{casestudy}) \\
Artefact & In design science, the constructed thing: a construct, model, method
  or instantiation. In reproducibility, the deposited data and code
  (\chref{designscience}, \chref{reproducibility}) \\
Audit trail & The dated record of data, coding decisions, memos and revisions
  that makes a qualitative analysis checkable (\chref{qda}) \\
Bibliographic coupling & Two papers linked because they cite the same earlier
  work. Shows current research fronts (\chref{bibliometrics}) \\
Carryover effect & In a within-subjects design, the effect of having already
  experienced one treatment on performance under the next
  (\chref{experiments}) \\
Cliff's $\delta$ & A distribution-free effect size for two groups; the default
  choice for software engineering data (\chref{sampling}) \\
Co-citation & Two papers linked because a later paper cites both. Shows the
  settled intellectual base (\chref{bibliometrics}) \\
Collider & A common effect of two variables. Conditioning on one creates a
  spurious association between them (\chref{regression}) \\
Common method bias & Correlation produced by the measurement method rather than
  the constructs --- typically one respondent, one instrument, one sitting
  (\chref{sem}) \\
Confounder & A variable causing both the treatment and the outcome. Must be
  controlled, or the estimate is biased (\chref{questions}) \\
Construct & The abstract thing of interest, which cannot be observed directly
  (\chref{measurement}) \\
Construct validity & Whether the operationalisation captures the intended
  construct rather than a neighbouring one (\chref{validity}) \\
Contextual integrity & Whether a use of data matches the norms of the context
  it came from. The test for observational data ethics (\chref{ethics}) \\
Counterbalancing & Distributing treatment order systematically so order is not
  confounded with treatment (\chref{experiments}) \\
CRediT & A 14-role taxonomy making individual contributions to a paper explicit
  (\chref{integrity}) \\
Data leakage & Use of information during training or model selection that would
  not be available at prediction time (\chref{mleval}) \\
Delta & The difference between what was known before your work and after. What
  ``novelty'' means operationally (\chref{contribution}) \\
Dependability & The qualitative analogue of reliability, established by an
  audit trail (\chref{qda}) \\
Design knowledge & What building and evaluating an artefact taught, stated
  separately from the artefact so it transfers (\chref{designscience}) \\
Difference-in-differences & Comparing the change in treated units with the
  change in untreated units. Removes time-invariant unmeasured differences
  (\chref{quasiexp}) \\
Ecological validity & Whether results hold in realistic settings
  (\chref{validity}) \\
Exaptation & Extending a known solution to a new problem domain. A contribution
  only if the transfer was non-obvious (\chref{designscience}) \\
Experimental unit & The thing assigned to a treatment. Randomise it, count it,
  analyse at its level (\chref{experiments}) \\
Falsifier & What you could observe that would force you to withdraw your claim.
  An empty falsifier means no project (\chref{contribution}) \\
FAIR & Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable. Accessible does not mean
  open (\chref{reproducibility}) \\
FEDS & A framework choosing evaluation strategy from risk, along
  formative--summative and artificial--naturalistic axes
  (\chref{designscience}) \\
FINER & Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant. Five tests a research
  question must pass (\chref{questions}) \\
Forking paths & The analyses you would have run had the data differed. They
  count towards multiplicity even if never performed (\chref{inference}) \\
Grey literature & Material outside commercial and academic publishing: reports,
  standards, blogs, documentation, theses (\chref{greylit}) \\
HARKing & Presenting a hypothesis suggested by the data as if predicted in
  advance (\chref{prereg}) \\
HJRS & The HEC Journal Recognition System, source of the W, X, Y categories
  \S15 refers to (\chref{publication}) \\
HTMT & Heterotrait-monotrait ratio; the current standard test of discriminant
  validity (\chref{sem}) \\
Identification strategy & The argument that your comparison approximates the
  counterfactual. Not a technique (\chref{quasiexp}) \\
Information power & A justification for qualitative sample size based on the
  aim's narrowness, sample specificity and theoretical support
  (\chref{sampling}) \\
Instantiation & A working system embodying a construct, model or method
  (\chref{designscience}) \\
Internal validity & Whether the observed relationship is causal
  (\chref{validity}) \\
Latin square & An ordering scheme in which each treatment appears once in each
  position, balancing order and material (\chref{experiments}) \\
Likert item / scale & A single agreement statement (ordinal) versus several
  summed to measure one construct (treated as interval)
  (\chref{measurement}) \\
Mediator & A variable on the causal path from treatment to outcome. Controlling
  for it removes the effect you wanted to measure (\chref{regression}) \\
Meta-inference & The conclusion drawn from integrating mixed-methods strands,
  which neither alone supports (\chref{mixed}) \\
Mono-operation bias & Using a single task, system or instantiation of a
  treatment (\chref{validity}) \\
Multivocal literature review & A review including both academic and
  practitioner sources (\chref{greylit}) \\
Negative case analysis & Actively seeking data contradicting your theme.
  Falsification in qualitative form (\chref{qda}) \\
Nonresponse bias & Systematic difference between responders and non-responders
  on the measured variables. Not the same as response rate (\chref{survey}) \\
Operationalisation & The explicit rule turning an abstract construct into a
  recorded observation. A claim, and it can be wrong (\chref{questions}) \\
Optional stopping & Collecting data until the result is significant. Guarantees
  significance under the null if you keep looking (\chref{prereg}) \\
Overdispersion & Count data with variance exceeding the mean. Requires negative
  binomial rather than Poisson (\chref{regression}) \\
p-hacking & Trying analytic variants until one is significant
  (\chref{prereg}) \\
p-value & The probability of data at least this extreme if the null hypothesis
  and all other model assumptions held (\chref{inference}) \\
PICOC & Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, Context. Decomposes a
  question and drives the search string (\chref{questions}) \\
Pre-registration & A time-stamped public statement of hypotheses, design,
  sample size and analysis, deposited before data collection
  (\chref{prereg}) \\
PRISMA & The reporting standard for systematic reviews, including the flow
  diagram (\chref{slr}) \\
Proposition & In case study research, what you expect to find and why. Directs
  attention; not tested statistically (\chref{casestudy}) \\
Purposive sampling & Selecting participants because they can inform the
  question. The qualitative norm (\chref{qualitative}) \\
Registered report & A two-stage format in which the design is reviewed and
  accepted in principle before data collection (\chref{prereg}) \\
Reliability & Consistency of measurement. Necessary but not sufficient for
  validity (\chref{measurement}) \\
Replicability & A different team obtaining the finding using their own
  artefacts (\chref{reproducibility}) \\
Reproducibility & A different team obtaining the result using the author's
  artefacts. A floor, not a goal (\chref{reproducibility}) \\
Salami slicing & Dividing one study into several minimal papers
  (\chref{integrity}) \\
Saturation & The point at which additional qualitative data yields no new
  codes. Must be demonstrated, not asserted (\chref{qualitative}) \\
SESOI & Smallest effect size of interest: the smallest effect that would matter
  to anyone. What you power for (\chref{sampling}) \\
Similarity index & The percentage of matched text in an originality report. Not
  a plagiarism verdict (\chref{integrity}) \\
Snowballing & Following citations backward and forward from a start set to find
  relevant work a search string missed (\chref{greylit}) \\
Text recycling & Reusing your own text in a new work. Tolerated in methods,
  not in results (\chref{integrity}) \\
Theoretical replication & Selecting cases that differ on a dimension your
  theory says matters, predicting different results. A genuine test
  (\chref{casestudy}) \\
Thick description & Description detailed enough that a reader can interpret
  meaning and context, and judge transferability (\chref{qda}) \\
Threats to validity & Alternative explanations for your result, classified as
  conclusion, internal, construct or external (\chref{validity}) \\
TOST & Two one-sided tests; the procedure for claiming equivalence
  (\chref{inference}) \\
Triangulation & Using multiple sources, observers, methods or theories. Its
  purpose is to expose disagreement, not accumulate agreement
  (\chref{casestudy}) \\
Trustworthiness & The qualitative quality framework: credibility,
  transferability, dependability, confirmability (\chref{qda}) \\
Winner's curse & Systematic over-estimation of published effects caused by low
  power plus significance filtering (\chref{sampling}) \\
Within-subjects design & Every participant experiences every treatment. Buys
  power; costs carryover (\chref{experiments}) \\
\end{longtable}
\end{center}
