Before any method can be chosen, three things have to be settled: what
would count as a contribution, what you believe about how knowledge is
produced, and what exactly you are asking. Most failed graduate projects failed
here, months before the first line of code, and no amount of later
sophistication repairs them. This part is short and unglamorous, and it is the
part that decides whether the rest of the work has anywhere to go.

\begin{conceptbox}[What Part~1 Covers]
\begin{tight}
  \item \textbf{Chapter 1 --- What Counts as a Contribution.} Claim, evidence, falsifier; eight contribution types and what each owes its reader.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 2 --- Philosophy of Science.} The commitments your methods already make on your behalf, and how to keep them consistent.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 3 --- Research Questions and Constructs.} FINER, PICOC, operationalisation, and the alignment table that exposes an unanswerable question.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 4 --- The Publication System.} Where computing work belongs, what the regulations require of it, and how to avoid destroying it in a predatory venue.
\end{tight}
\end{conceptbox}
