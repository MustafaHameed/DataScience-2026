Everything to this point concerns getting an answer. This part concerns
whether anyone should believe it --- including you. Reproducibility,
pre-registration, ethics and integrity are usually taught as compliance, which
is why they are usually ignored. They are better understood as the practices
that make a finding survive contact with a sceptic. The rules in this part are
also the ones you can be failed on without ever having been taught them, so
they are quoted here exactly.

\begin{conceptbox}[What Part~5 Covers]
\begin{tight}
  \item \textbf{Chapter 25 --- Reproducibility and Artefact Evaluation.} Badging, containers, seeds, FAIR data, and packaging work so it survives you.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 26 --- Pre-registration and Questionable Practices.} HARKing, p-hacking, forking paths, publication bias, and registered reports.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 27 --- Research Ethics and Human Subjects.} Consent, data protection, re-identification risk, and the ethics of scraping and mining.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 28 --- Academic Integrity and the Similarity Test.} The numeric thresholds that bind you, how to read an originality report, and authorship.
  \item \textbf{Chapter 29 --- Generative AI in the Research Process.} The IUB position, read exactly; what is permitted, what is prohibited, and how to disclose.
\end{tight}
\end{conceptbox}
