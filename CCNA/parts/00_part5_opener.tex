\vspace*{0.4cm}
This part looks like a miscellany and is not. It finishes two things at once: the
three topics of domain~1 that had no natural home earlier --- radio, clients and
virtual infrastructure --- and the whole of domain~5, which is about
\emph{operating} a network rather than building one.

Domain~5 is only 10\% of the exam, and it is entirely new. Nothing in it existed
under v1.1 in this form: agentic AI, prompt selection, and the five management
approaches are all v2.0 additions, and SNMP and syslog have been re-cast around
\emph{interpreting} output rather than configuring it.

\begin{examnote}[Ten per cent, and the cheapest marks in the exam]
Domain~5 has six topics and almost no configuration. Four of them --- \tp{5.1},
\tp{5.2}, \tp{5.3} and \tp{5.4} --- are \emph{describe} or \emph{select}
questions answerable from a page of well-organised facts. \tp{5.6} asks you to
\emph{interpret} a syslog line, which is a skill you can acquire in an afternoon
and keep for a career. Only \tp{5.5}, Ansible, asks you to \emph{use} anything.

Candidates routinely under-prepare this domain because it is the smallest and
because ``AI'' sounds unexaminable. It is 10\% of the paper for perhaps 5\% of
the study effort of domain~2. Do not leave it until the last week.
\end{examnote}

\begin{conceptbox}[What ``agentic'' is doing in a CCNA blueprint]
Topic~\tp{5.1} names \term{agentic AI} specifically, and \tp{5.2} asks you to
choose a \emph{prompt}. That is unusual for a Cisco exam and it is worth being
straight about why it is there: network operations tooling now routinely includes
systems that observe, suggest and --- increasingly --- act.

Chapter~32 treats this as an operational subject with real limits rather than a
marketing one. The examinable content is small and specific: what distinguishes
an agentic system from a generative one, and the four prompt components the
blueprint names by hand.
\end{conceptbox}
